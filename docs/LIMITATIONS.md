# Ledgerwatch: known limitations

All results below are from synthetic data generated by the same author as the engine.
They show what the engine does on data shaped like what it was built for, and on a
deliberately hostile dataset. They say nothing yet about a real client's files.

## Measured results

| Dataset | Planted exceptions | Found | Missed | False alarms | Wrong links |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clean synthetic | 44 | 44 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Hostile synthetic | 58 | 46 | 12 | 20 | 0 |

Amounts at risk were exact to the kobo for every exception found.

## Where the engine asks a human instead of guessing (hostile dataset)

- Orders with identical amounts and dates whose references are mangled: reported as
  "missing settlement" because the engine will not guess which line belongs to which order.
- A settlement that arrives more than 14 days late with a mangled reference: not linked.
- A wrong settled amount combined with a mangled reference: not linked.
- A duplicate where both copies have mangled references: the engine will not pick the real
  one, so the duplicate is not reported.
- A bank credit with the same amount and date as a real payout: both credits are flagged.
- A bank payout that is short of its batch total: reported as an orphan credit, because the
  engine has no "shortfall" exception type yet.

## Known gaps in the product

- Settlement lines that cannot be explained are counted but do not yet appear as exceptions
  a reviewer can see. A "needs review" queue is planned.
- Fee rates and the settlement window are fixed for one fictional processor (PayRail).
  Real processors need their own adapters and agreed rates.
- Only one processor format and one bank format exist, both fictional. No real Paystack,
  Flutterwave, or bank export format has been tested.
- Single currency (NGN), credits only, no refunds or chargebacks.
- No authentication yet. Not production-ready and makes no compliance claims.
